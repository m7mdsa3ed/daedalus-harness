Prototypes

Static, whole-page options for the two surfaces being redesigned. Each is built on the app's own tokens and drawn from one fake workspace, so a difference between two of them is a difference in the design under test — not in the chrome around it. Nothing here imports the client.

Press d on any page to flip light/dark.

Transcript layouts

While the sidebar options argue about what the left column holds, these argue about the transcript itself: what a turn is, where the work goes, and where you type. T1 is the layout shipped today; every other option is a single change to it, and the option states what it costs.

Ten options: T1 is the shipped layout, T2–T7 are the original set, and T9–T10 are the newest — a prompt-anchored grid and a stripped-back reading view.

T1

Current column

The baseline

The shipped layout: one 748px column, your prompt a tinted bubble at 80%, the agent's prose bare on the ground, each step one 24px line, and the composer docked in glass over the foot. Nothing to choose here.
T2

Prose feed

Hide the work

Every tool call, thought and run folds to one mark on a left rail; prose gets larger and the column a little narrower. A long thread reads as a document.

Costs — you cannot see what it touched without opening it.
T3

Turns as cards

The turn is the unit

Each exchange is one card on a quiet ground: your prompt is the header, the work is the body, and what it cost and touched is the footer.

Costs — a card per turn is a lot of shape for a two-line exchange.
T4

Work first

The inverse of T2

The actions get the column — a wide rail, foreground step titles, diffs open as they fall — while the agent's prose steps back to a caption's weight.

Costs — the conversation stops being the thing you read.
T5

Two panes

A margin for the thread

A 256px right column holds what is about the thread — the live plan, the files this turn touched, the context gauge, the queue — while the transcript shrinks beside it.

Costs — the panel has to be wide; below ~1100px the margin folds away.
T6

Inline work

Steps in the gutter

The work moves into the left gutter as a branch of marks with no titles; only an edit's diff, a command's output or a thought claims the column. The step's name is a tooltip.

Costs — a long file name is unreadable without opening the step.
T7

Threaded

Your prompt as a heading

The bubble becomes a node on a spine: your words are a title and a time, and everything the agent did hangs beneath them. The order of the conversation is the page's indentation.

Costs — your words stop looking like speech; the thread is a log.
T8

Focus

The composer in flow

A narrower column, larger prose, the work folded to one line per run, and the composer sitting in the transcript at the point the next message lands rather than docked over it.

Costs — the box moves as the thread grows; no glass to rest content behind.
T9

Prompt anchored

Your prompt is a column

Your message gets a fixed column on the left and everything it caused runs down the rest, aligned with it. Two turns stack as two such rows.

Costs — the column is spent on the prompt, and the answer gets the smaller share.
T10

Minimal

The conversation only

No sidebar, no tabs, no card, no footer, no timestamps. Your prompt is one line under a rule, a step is a bare icon, and the composer is one hairline at the foot.

Costs — every reading the app has is somewhere else or nowhere.

Composer layouts

The transcript's other half. The unit has three parts — the shelf (a pile of cards: the front one open, the rest a 14px sliver), the card (the textarea over its toolbar row) and the footer (one row, always drawn — the project, the tools, the readings). C1 is the unit as it ships; the options move the shelf, the rows and the measure.

C1

Current composer

The baseline

The shipped unit: the shelf as a pile, the frosted card at one measure, the scope row as a permanent footer, and the readings clustered at its end. Annotated; nothing to choose.
C2

Command bar

Everything on one row

Text, model, readings and Send share one 32px line. The composer for someone who types one sentence at a time.

Costs — a long message scrolls sideways; a busy shelf does not fit.
C3

Composer panel

Named tabs

The pile becomes a header row: Queue, Files, Plan and History are named, counted tabs of the card, and the readings move into the footer.

Costs — a row of chrome for what a pile answers by being looked at.
C4

Composer in flow

Docked no more

The card sits at the end of the transcript instead of pinned over its foot, so nothing you read is under glass and the transcript reserves nothing.

Costs — the box moves as the thread grows; the working line can fall below the fold.

Tab strip layouts

How the header names what is open. Every variant keeps the app's two rules: no tab wears a colour (the reading is a dot, and the title is drawn under a fade when the controls appear), and a narrow strip keeps the tab you are on and moves the rest to a list.

Tabs-A

Current

The three weights, shown

The shipped strip, then the tab's resting, hover and active states laid out side by side so the material can be judged without pointing at anything.
Tabs-B

Two rows

Tabs, then where you are

A second row under the tabs carries the panel's own path and controls — a file's path, a page's sections — so a title stops carrying the path. Click a tab to switch it.

Costs — 24px on every group, including the panels with nothing to put there.
Tabs-C

Breadcrumb

One location, not tabs

The strip becomes a path — project / thread / Plan — with ancestors clickable and no tab to close, because a place is not a thing you keep.

Costs — one place at a time: no docked terminal, no second thread, no split.

Sidebar layouts

The earlier set. They differ in information architecture, not in styling — what gets a fixed band, what folds, what is a rail, and which question the column answers first.

v2

One index

Recents as a fold

Pins as chips, no “Opened” tier, one flat index with the project tree under it. The smallest change to today's sidebar.
v3

Activity rail

Status is a place

A 52px rail picks what the list shows — Threads, Projects, Needs you — and the list is that answer at full height.
v10

Sticky sections

v2, for a long list

v2's one index with every tier heading pinned as it scrolls, and a “Needs you” strip above the scroll that never moves.
v11

Compact

v2, at density

The same index drawn tight: 26px rows, no tier gaps, and the fixed nav as one icon row instead of seven — roughly seven more threads on screen.
v12

Organized

v2, with a switch

Threads and Projects stop sharing one scroll: the header swaps the list, so the project tree gets the whole viewport when it is what you asked for.
v13

Two-line rows

v2, with a bigger row

Every thread gets a title line and a meta line — project and time, or the question it is waiting on. Built for titles like the long one in this very workspace.
v14

Quiet chrome

v2, with less furniture

The brand row, new-thread button, search row and list heading collapse into one bar: the mark, then new / search / filter as three icons.
v15

Section cards

v2, with section surfaces

Each tier becomes a card with its heading and count inside it, so the column is a countable set of sections rather than one flat list with text in it.
v4

Projects first

The tree is the sidebar

Every thread lives under its project. No flat recent list at all — the Codex / Cursor model.
v5

Status board

Needs you, first

Two full groups — Needs you and Running — with the action on the row itself, then everything else. Projects fold at the bottom.
v6

Timeline

Strictly chronological

One spine, newest first, period headings, the project a colour on each row. No folders anywhere.
v7

Pinned bands

Middle ground

Needs you and Running as two fixed bands that never scroll away, over one dense, quiet index of everything else.
v8

Omni-search

Type, don't scroll

A filter box and project chips over one flat ranked list. For a workspace where forty threads is normal.
v9

Two panes

The info card, permanent

The index on the left, what is about the selected row on the right — status, project, model, actions. Needs a 360px sidebar.
